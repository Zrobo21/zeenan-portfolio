Most on-page SEO checklists online are recycled from five years ago, padded with advice that either doesn't matter anymore or never mattered much. Here's what actually earns its place on a real checklist right now.

## Title tag: specific beats clever

Your title tag should tell both Google and the searcher exactly what the page covers, with the primary keyword placed naturally near the front. "Everything You Need" is clever but tells a search engine nothing. "On-Page SEO Checklist for Beginners" tells it everything.

## One clear H1, then a logical H2/H3 structure

A page should have exactly one H1 that mirrors the title's intent. After that, headers should read like an outline someone could skim and still understand the page's structure, not just be there to break up text visually.

## First 100 words carry weight

Search engines and readers both form a quick judgment from the opening lines. The primary keyword should appear naturally within this section, and the paragraph should state clearly what the reader will get from staying on the page.

## Internal links need actual purpose

Linking to another page on your site just to have internal links doesn't help much. Link when it genuinely helps the reader go deeper on something you've only touched briefly — and use descriptive anchor text ("read our keyword research guide") instead of "click here."

## Image alt text describes, it doesn't stuff

Alt text exists primarily for accessibility — describing an image to someone using a screen reader. A side benefit is that search engines read it too. Write what the image actually shows; don't cram keywords into it that have nothing to do with the picture.

## Page speed is still a real ranking factor

A page that takes six seconds to load loses visitors before they read a word, and search engines notice that pattern too. Compressing images and avoiding unnecessary heavy scripts remains one of the highest-impact, lowest-effort fixes available.

## Mobile experience isn't optional anymore

The majority of searches, especially in Bangladesh, happen on phones. A page that requires pinch-zooming or has buttons too small to tap accurately will lose rankings and visitors regardless of how good the content is.

## Meta description doesn't affect rankings directly, but it affects clicks

Google doesn't use your meta description as a ranking signal, but it often uses it as the snippet text in search results. A meta description that clearly promises value earns more clicks, and click-through rate does correlate with how pages perform over time.

## FAQ

**How long should a blog post be for good SEO?**
There's no universal number. Length should match what's needed to genuinely answer the search intent — a quick factual question needs a short, direct answer; a comparison or how-to guide often needs more depth. Padding a short answer to hit a word count hurts more than it helps.

**Does keyword density still matter?**
Not in the old sense of hitting an exact percentage. What matters now is using the keyword and its natural variations where they fit, so the page reads naturally while still clearly signaling its topic.

**Is it bad to have multiple keywords targeting the same page?**
Closely related keywords with the same search intent can share a page well. Keywords with genuinely different intent (like "what is SEO" versus "hire SEO expert Dhaka") usually deserve separate pages.

**How often should I update old content for SEO?**
Revisit high-traffic or high-potential pages every six to twelve months. Outdated statistics, broken links, or stale examples are worth fixing even on pages that are already ranking reasonably well.
