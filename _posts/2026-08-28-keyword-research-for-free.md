Everyone tells you keyword research needs Ahrefs or SEMrush. It doesn't. Those tools make the job faster, sure, but the actual signals they're built on — what people search, how often, and what else they search alongside it — are sitting right there in Google, free, if you know where to look.

## Start with Google's autocomplete

Type your topic into Google and stop typing. The suggestions that pop up aren't random — they're real queries other people have searched recently, ranked by how common they are. Type "digital marketing" and you'll see variations like "digital marketing course," "digital marketing salary," "digital marketing agency near me." Each of those is a keyword idea with built-in proof that people search it.

Go one step further: add a letter after your phrase. "Digital marketing a," "digital marketing b," and so on through the alphabet. This surfaces long-tail variations you'd never think of on your own.

## People Also Ask is a goldmine

Search your main topic and scroll to the "People also ask" box. Click on one question and two or three more appear. This isn't just for finding blog topics — every question in that box is a real thing people type into Google, meaning it's a validated keyword with clear intent behind it.

## Related searches at the bottom of the page

Scroll all the way down on any Google search results page. The "related searches" section shows queries connected to your topic that Google's own algorithm considers relevant. This is especially useful for spotting seasonal or regional variations — searches specific to Bangladesh often show up here that generic keyword tools miss entirely.

## Check what's already ranking

Search your target keyword and actually read the top 5 results. Not skim — read. Note what subtopics they all cover (that's the baseline you need to match) and what none of them cover well (that's your opportunity). This single step tells you more about real search intent than any volume number could.

## Free tools worth using alongside this

Google Trends shows you whether interest in a topic is rising or falling over time, and lets you compare regions — useful for checking if a keyword is actually relevant to Bangladesh or just trending globally. Google Search Console, once your site has some traffic, shows you the exact queries people already use to find you, which is the most accurate keyword data you'll ever get because it's about your actual audience.

## FAQ

**Do I really not need a paid tool at all?**
For early-stage keyword research, no. Paid tools add search volume numbers and difficulty scores, which help you prioritize faster once you have dozens of keywords. But the actual discovery process works fine without them.

**How many keywords should I research before writing content?**
Aim for a cluster of 10-15 related keywords per topic rather than one keyword in isolation. This lets you naturally cover subtopics in a single well-structured article instead of writing ten thin pages.

**How do I know if a keyword is too competitive for a new site?**
If the top 5 results are all large, established brands or government/news sites, it's going to be hard to outrank them soon. Look for keywords where at least one or two of the top results are small businesses or lesser-known blogs — that's a sign the keyword is winnable.

**Should I target Bangla or English keywords?**
Check both. Many Bangladeshi searchers type in English even when speaking Bangla day to day, especially for business and tech topics, but local service searches ("kache SEO service" style queries) often mix Bangla-English. Search both versions and see which actually returns relevant Bangladesh-specific results.
