Big brands can afford to throw money at problems until something works. Small businesses can't, and most marketing advice online is written for the ones who can. Here's what actually fits a limited budget.

## Owned channels before paid channels

Before spending a single taka on ads, make sure the free channels are actually working. A Facebook page with an incomplete about section, no recent posts, and no Google Business Profile is leaking potential customers for free — fixing that costs nothing but time.

## Hyperlocal targeting beats broad reach

A shop in Dhanmondi doesn't need to reach all of Dhaka. Targeting a tight radius around the actual customer base, whether through boosted posts with location targeting or through partnerships with nearby businesses, stretches a small budget much further than broad targeting ever will.

## Customer photos are free content

Asking satisfied customers to share a photo with their purchase, then reposting it with permission, produces content that feels more trustworthy than anything a business could produce about itself — at zero production cost.

## Cross-promotion with neighboring businesses

A tailor and a fabric shop on the same street have overlapping customers but aren't competitors. Simple arrangements — recommending each other, sharing a small discount code, tagging each other in posts — cost nothing and reach an audience already primed to trust local recommendations.

## WhatsApp Business as a retention tool

Many Bangladeshi customers already prefer WhatsApp for inquiries. Setting up WhatsApp Business with a proper catalog, quick replies, and order updates costs nothing and turns one-time buyers into repeat customers through direct, low-friction communication.

## Micro-influencers over big names

A local micro-influencer with two or three thousand genuinely engaged local followers often delivers better results for a small budget than paying for a single post from someone with a huge but generic following. Local relevance matters more than raw numbers here.

## Consistency compounds — bursts don't

A shop that posts inconsistently, disappearing for weeks then posting five times in one day, builds far less trust than one that shows up reliably twice a week. Consistency is entirely free and often the single biggest lever small businesses underuse.

## FAQ

**What's the minimum budget needed to see any results from paid ads?**
Even a very small daily budget can produce learnings if targeting is precise and tracked properly. The mistake most small businesses make isn't spending too little — it's spending without a clear goal or without tracking what happened afterward.

**Should I focus on Facebook or Instagram for a small local business?**
Whichever platform your actual existing and potential customers use most. For many local shops in Bangladesh, Facebook remains the stronger channel, though this varies by age group and product category.

**How do I get customers to actually leave reviews or share photos?**
Ask directly, and make it easy — a simple reminder at checkout or a follow-up message works better than hoping customers do it on their own.

**Is it worth hiring someone for social media if the budget is very tight?**
Not necessarily at first. Many of these tactics can be run by the business owner directly with an hour or two a week. Hiring help makes more sense once the volume of work grows beyond what the owner can sustain alongside running the business.
