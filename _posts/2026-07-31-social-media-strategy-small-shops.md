Most social media strategy guides are written as if the reader has a marketing team, a content calendar tool, and three hours a day to spend on it. Small shop owners have none of that, and pretending otherwise is why so many strategies get abandoned within a month.

## Pick two platforms, not five

Trying to maintain a strong presence on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and more all at once with limited time usually means all of them get mediocre effort. Choosing the two platforms where the actual target customers spend time, and doing those well, beats spreading thin across everything.

## Batch content instead of daily improvising

Sitting down once a week to plan and shoot content for the week, rather than trying to think of something new every single day, is far more sustainable. Even thirty minutes on a slow day can produce enough photos and captions for the week ahead.

## Content pillars remove the guesswork

Instead of wondering what to post each day, decide on three or four repeating content types in advance — product features, behind-the-scenes, customer stories, and simple tips related to the product. Rotating through these pillars removes decision fatigue and keeps content varied without needing constant new ideas.

## Respond before you post more

A page that posts frequently but leaves comments and messages unanswered for days is actively losing trust. Prioritizing timely responses over posting volume protects the relationships that already exist before chasing new reach.

## Track only what changes your decisions

Vanity metrics like follower count feel good but rarely tell you what to do next. Tracking which specific posts led to actual inquiries or sales, even manually by asking new customers how they found the shop, gives far more useful direction.

## Seasonal planning prevents last-minute scrambling

Knowing in advance which occasions matter for a specific business (Eid, Pohela Boishakh, wedding season, back-to-school) and planning content around those a few weeks ahead avoids the rushed, generic posts that go out when there's no lead time.

## FAQ

**How many times a week should a small business post?**
Two to four times a week, consistently, generally outperforms daily posting that isn't sustainable and eventually stops. Consistency over months matters more than frequency in any single week.

**Should Reels or short videos be a priority even for a simple product-based shop?**
Short video content tends to get better organic reach right now across most platforms, but it's not mandatory. A shop that can realistically produce simple videos should try it; one that can't should focus on doing photo and text content well rather than producing poor-quality video out of obligation.

**Is it worth paying for a social media scheduling tool?**
For very small operations, native scheduling within Facebook and Instagram's own tools is often enough. Paid scheduling tools become more useful once posting volume or the number of platforms grows.

**How do I know if my social media strategy is actually working?**
Look past likes and comments to whether actual inquiries, orders, or foot traffic can be traced back to social media, even through simple methods like asking customers directly or using unique discount codes tied to specific platforms.
