Walk through any commercial area in Dhaka and most shops have a Facebook page. Far fewer have a claimed, optimized Google Business Profile. That gap is exactly why it's one of the easiest wins left for local businesses willing to spend an hour setting it up properly.

## Claim it before someone else's version wins

Google sometimes auto-generates basic listings from map data even if the owner never created one. Search your business name first — if a listing already exists unclaimed, claim it rather than creating a duplicate, which confuses both Google and customers.

## Category selection is not decorative

The primary category you choose directly affects which searches your business shows up for. A restaurant categorized as "Restaurant" instead of the more specific "Bangladeshi Restaurant" is competing in a much broader, harder pool than necessary. Choose the most specific accurate category, and add secondary categories only where genuinely relevant.

## Complete every section, not just the required ones

Business hours, phone number, website link, service area, and a written description all factor into how complete and trustworthy your profile appears. An incomplete profile signals lower credibility to both Google's ranking system and to customers comparing options.

## Photos aren't just for looks

Listings with regularly updated, genuine photos of the actual business — not stock images — tend to get more engagement and trust. Photos of the storefront, products, and interior help potential customers self-select before they even visit, which reduces wasted walk-ins and improves satisfaction for the ones who do come.

## Reviews and how you respond to them

Reviews influence local rankings, but the response matters almost as much as the review itself. Responding to both positive and negative reviews, specifically and without generic copy-paste replies, shows active management and builds trust with anyone reading the reviews later.

## Posts keep the profile active

Google Business Profile has its own posting feature, separate from Facebook or Instagram, that lets you share updates, offers, or products directly on the listing. Profiles that post regularly tend to appear more active and relevant compared to static, never-updated listings.

## Questions and Answers section shapes first impressions

Anyone can ask a public question on a Google Business Profile, and anyone can answer it — including random users, not just the owner. Proactively adding common questions and answering them yourself prevents incorrect or outdated answers from strangers being the first thing a potential customer sees.

## FAQ

**Is Google Business Profile really free?**
Yes, completely free to claim, verify, and manage. There's no paid tier required for the core features that matter most for local visibility.

**How long does verification take?**
It varies — some businesses get verified within a few days through phone or email, while postcard verification (mail sent to the business address) can take one to two weeks.

**Does my business need a physical address to use this?**
Not necessarily. Service-area businesses without a public-facing storefront can still create a profile and hide the exact address while listing the areas they serve.

**How often should I post updates on the profile?**
Once or twice a week is a reasonable baseline. What matters more than frequency is that posts are genuinely relevant — new offers, seasonal products, or updates customers would actually want to know.
